Planning a spontaneous evening usually means opening several venue pages, then comparing hours, prices, locations, and walking time by hand.

Serendipity turns that work into one source-backed Shibuya plan. A person chooses a date, time window, reference budget, interests, and a walking limit. The result contains two or three real places. Every stop shows its address, published opening window, reference price, estimated walk, and official link.

Nothing here pretends to be a live booking. The price covers only the listed admission or activity, and every important fact opens to its evidence and comparison date.

WebMCP is useful because an AI assistant can collaborate on this same page without scraping the interface. The top-level page exposes five validated actions. The agent finds a plan, opens evidence for a specific place, and asks for a one-stop replacement.

The replacement keeps the other stops fixed and visibly recalculates the reference total, walking burden, and downstream times. Saving is explicit, browser-local, and never contacts a venue.

Humans and agents use the same controller, while unsupported requests return an honest no-result instead of unrelated filler.

Serendipity is a verifiable plan you can act on—not another technical proof of concept.
