# Demo video script — target 2:20

**Status**: Draft; do not record the Site Tools segment until Available Site
Tools shows exactly five Hub tools.  
**Hard limit**: 2:59; target 2:20 leaves recovery margin.  
**Language**: English narration; no copyrighted music or third-party marks.

## Pre-record checklist

- Confirm fixed production aliases and deployment IDs.
- Run the protected reset and one read-only search.
- Use a fresh browser page with Site Tools enabled and Sol or Terra.
- Open Available Site Tools and confirm exactly two read and three write tools.
- Hide profiles, notifications, account history, headers, secrets, Vercel, and
  Supabase panels.
- Keep a manual-fallback capture available only as an honest resilience cutaway,
  not as WebMCP evidence.

## Timeline and narration

### 0:00–0:15 — Real problem

**Visual**: Initial Serendipity page; four large moods, one action, three Provider
identities.

**Narration**:

> A free urban evening sounds simple until you reconcile live availability,
> travel time, and budget across separate venue websites. Serendipity turns that
> fragmented work into one safe, reversible route.

### 0:15–0:32 — Why WebMCP

**Visual**: Browser Available Site Tools showing the five top-level Hub tools.

**Narration**:

> The top-level Hub exposes five focused Site Tools: find and show are read-only;
> hold, confirm, and release are explicit mutations. The three venues remain
> independent sites and keep their inventory authority.

### 0:32–0:58 — Agent discovery

**Prompt**:

> Find a surprising evening in the supported Shibuya network after 6 p.m., under
> five thousand yen, and show the best route. Do not hold or confirm anything.

**Visual**: ChatGPT invokes find_serendipity_options; Kiln/Nori/Loop change to
Found; route, total, time, and travel gaps render.

**Narration**:

> One read-only tool calls all three Provider APIs in parallel. A deterministic
> engine rejects partial or infeasible combinations and returns only complete
> routes. Search never reserves inventory.

### 0:58–1:28 — Reversible hold

**Prompt**:

> Hold this route for ninety seconds.

**Visual**: hold_bundle; all three cards become Held; countdown appears.

**Narration**:

> Hold is a separate mutation. If one Provider fails, successful holds are
> compensated. Private Provider tokens are encrypted server-side; the agent and
> activity view receive only safe references.

### 1:28–1:48 — Explicit confirmation

**Prompt**:

> I checked the total. Confirm the demo reservation.

**Visual**: confirm_bundle; three Confirmed cards and three-reference receipt.

**Narration**:

> Confirmation happens only after explicit intent. Unknown outcomes are
> reconciled before the page claims success. This is a demo, and no payment or
> real venue booking occurs.

### 1:48–2:08 — Visible proof

**Visual**: Expand proof; exact origins, Provider pages, route proof, and Site
Tool activity with source/origin/duration/correlation. Briefly show Recently Used.

**Narration**:

> The proof is part of the product: three real origins, the composed route, and
> sanitized activity. It says Site Tool only when a Site Tool ran; unsupported
> browsers get the same workflow through a clearly labeled manual fallback.

### 2:08–2:20 — Impact and honest boundary

**Visual**: Initial UI and Shibuya launch network copy; optional architecture
diagram overlay owned by the project.

**Narration**:

> Shibuya is the validated launch network. The model generalizes through gated
> area data packs, but no city appears before origins, travel data, reset,
> reliability, and production tests pass. Serendipity demonstrates an open web
> where people and agents can coordinate without centralizing every site.

## Failure rule

If the real Site Tools workflow fails during recording, stop. Do not substitute
a manual click, splice a mocked result, or label Chrome diagnostics as ChatGPT
evidence. Preserve the failed run, repair or resolve rollout, reset, and record a
new continuous workflow.
